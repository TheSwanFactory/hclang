# Denum / Cayley–Dickson Space Elevator: first feasibility gate

This is an **experimental diagnostic**, not an implementation of the audited
Space Elevator theorem (Explore 016, especially T04), and not a new HC observer.

The existing HC Denum has a Count and nonnegative channel loads. The shipped
KDSL observer (`.###%%%`) uses Count as rank, aligns loads to 2^rank slots,
and subtracts antipodal slots to read signed coordinates. The standalone
`denumSignedCoordinates` reproduces that coordinate rule on exact integers
for small ranks without modifying HC.

The new probe demonstrates a concrete **underdetermination**:

1. Two signed vectors are read from nonnegative Denum loads.
2. The same vectors admit the supplied recursive Cayley–Dickson product and
   a different, coordinatewise product.
3. Their commutator incidences disagree. Hence a phase-sensitive relation
   cannot be reconstructed from those coordinates *alone*.

The comparison uses the existing experimental Cayley–Dickson reference in
`cayley-dickson.ts`. That product is supplied, not generated by Denum
construction. The commutator is a diagnostic, **not** the curvature cable
defined and audited in Explore 016.

## Next constructive gate

To claim the Space Elevator as a Denum observation, a future change must:

- specify the exact audited curvature relation and its equivalence notion;
- construct the required phase-sensitive relation from HC/Denum-native
  operations without calling a supplied Cayley–Dickson product or lookup table;
- demonstrate intrinsic floor reconstruction and its symmetries against
  the Explore 016 T04/T06 witnesses, including boundary counterexamples.

Until then the strongest result is a *negative control*: KDSL coordinates
provide a carrier, but do not by themselves select a multiplication or cable.
